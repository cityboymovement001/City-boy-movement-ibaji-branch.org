Edo–Kogi Boundary Dispute Remains Unresolved as NBC Continues Demarcation Efforts

Fresh development highlights the continuing boundary process and concerns involving farmland between communities in Ibaji and neighbouring areas of Edo State.
IBAJI TV News Desk   |   September 28, 2026   |   5 min read
Edo–Kogi Boundary Demarcation Saga — Ibaji TV.

The long-running boundary dispute between Kogi and Edo States remains unresolved, with the National Boundary Commission continuing efforts to establish and physically demarcate the approved interstate boundary.

The latest development was disclosed by the Director-General of the National Boundary Commission, Adamu Adaji, in an interview with the News Agency of Nigeria in Abuja.

According to the NBC Director-General, the dispute has persisted partly because some of the physical landmarks referenced in the historical description of the boundary have disappeared over the years.

The National Boundary Commission says continued engagement with affected states and communities is necessary to address outstanding issues surrounding the boundary.

Historical Boundary

The National Boundary Commission says the Edo–Kogi boundary was originally defined under Legal Notice No. 126 of 1954.

Over the years, however, some physical features referenced in the historical description have disappeared, creating difficulties for technical experts attempting to accurately identify the boundary on the ground.

The Commission says technical and administrative efforts eventually produced an approved boundary line. However, attempts to physically establish the line encountered resistance from some communities that disputed whether the proposed alignment reflected their understanding of the traditional boundary.

Ibaji Farmland Concerns

The development is particularly important to the people of Ibaji Local Government Area of Kogi State.

Official information on the boundary process has identified farmland disputes involving communities in Ibaji and communities in Esan South-East Local Government Area of Edo State.

The farmland issue forms part of the wider Edo–Kogi boundary process and highlights the importance of finding a clearly established and mutually accepted boundary arrangement for affected communities.

The National Boundary Commission has continued to engage state governments, traditional institutions and affected communities as it works toward resolving the outstanding issues.

Demarcation Still Pending

The NBC Director-General said the Edo–Kogi boundary process remains ongoing and that the Commission intends to commence physical demarcation when the necessary resources become available.

He also acknowledged that some parties had presented additional documents, including court judgments that had not previously been brought to the attention of the Commission.

These documents are expected to be examined as part of the continuing process involving the affected states and communities.

Call for Peace and Cooperation

The National Boundary Commission has urged communities and other stakeholders along the boundary corridor to cooperate with the process and avoid actions capable of creating tension.

The Commission has also stressed the importance of protecting the legitimate property interests of people living in boundary communities.

Interstate boundaries are primarily intended for administrative purposes, while questions concerning individual or community property rights may require separate consideration.

What This Means for Ibaji

For communities in Ibaji, the ongoing boundary process is significant because farmland and other community interests may be affected by how the final boundary is interpreted and physically established.

Residents, traditional leaders and other stakeholders will therefore be watching closely as the National Boundary Commission continues consultations and prepares for eventual physical demarcation.

Any eventual agreement is expected to provide greater clarity for communities on both sides while helping to reduce disputes arising from competing interpretations of the boundary.
